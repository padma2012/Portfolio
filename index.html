The tools

Each one started as a question I couldn't answer from a pitch deck.

01 Sourcing signal

AI Capital Flows

A dashboard that tracks early-stage AI investment activity across Europe and the US, refreshing every morning at 7am. It pulls funding data and surfaces patterns by sector, stage, and geography — my primary sourcing signal for spotting companies and sub-sectors heating up before they hit mainstream deal flow.

  • Automated daily ingest, live by 7am
  • Pattern breakdowns across sector · stage · geography
  • Used to front-run consensus on emerging themes

02 First principles

LLM Chatbot, from scratch

I built a language-model chatbot from the ground up to understand how these systems actually work at the architecture level — not to ship a product, but to be able to reason about the models underneath the companies I evaluate. There's no better way to interrogate a technical claim than to have built the thing yourself.

03 Adoption proxy

Logo Velocity Tracker

A tracker that scans startup websites monthly to measure customer-logo growth over time. When revenue data isn't available — which, at the stage I invest, is most of the time — logo velocity becomes a defensible proxy for enterprise adoption and momentum.

  • Monthly automated scans of customer logos
  • Growth measured over time, not point-in-time
  • A proxy for adoption when revenue is opaque

The approach

I'd rather build the instrument than trust someone else's dashboard.

I spent years on the operating side and came out of an exit with a conviction that stuck: the best way to understand a company is to understand what it's actually made of. So I learned to code — properly, not superficially.

Each tool here answers a real gap in how early-stage AI gets evaluated. Where does capital concentrate before the narrative catches up? How do these models behave when you strip away the marketing? Is a company actually being adopted, or just funded? I don't outsource those questions anymore.

The result is diligence with fewer blind spots — and sourcing that runs a step ahead of the crowd.

  • Technical fluency — Python & SQL, self-taught, applied daily
  • Original signal — proprietary data, not consensus feeds
  • Operator's lens — built by someone who has run a company
